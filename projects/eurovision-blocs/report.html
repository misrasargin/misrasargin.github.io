Do Eurovision countries vote for their friends?

Method report for the Eurovision Conspiracy Board.

Summary

Using votes from grand finals ( to ), I measured how many more points each country gives another than the rest of Europe gives the same song. Of country pairs, show favouritism that survives a permutation test and a multiple-testing correction. Favouritism is mutual (r = ), it forms regional blocs, and on held-out years it comes mostly from the public vote ( points) and much less from juries ( points).

Data and cleaning

Source: the Eurovision Song Contest Dataset by Spijkervet and Burgoyne (release 2023), which records the points each country gave every other country. Cleaning steps, all in analysis/analyze.py:

  • Grand finals only. Semi-finals have different voters and would mix two contests.
  • From 1975, when the 1 to 8, 10 and 12 points system began. Earlier systems are not comparable.
  • The 2023 “rest of the world” vote is dropped, because it is not a country.
  • Since 2016 each country gives two sets of points (jury and public), so the maximum to one song is 24, not 12. I divide by the yearly maximum so all years share a 0 to 1 scale, then report results back in 12-point units.

Measuring bias

A raw count of points is misleading: everybody gave Sweden points in the years Sweden won. So I compare each vote with what the same song got from everyone else in the same year.

bias(A → B, year) = points A gave B − average points all other countries gave B

A pair’s bias is the average over every final where A voted and B performed. Pairs need at least such finals to be tested, which leaves pairs.

Is it more than chance?

The null hypothesis is that it does not matter who the voter is. Under it, the points a song received could be handed out to the voters in any order. So for each song in each year I shuffle the points between voters, recompute every pair’s bias, and repeat times. A pair’s p-value is the share of shuffles with a bias at least as large as the real one.

    Limitations

    • The analysis shows that favouritism exists, not what causes it. Language, diaspora, shared taste and politics are not separated.
    • Shuffling each song separately ignores the fact that one voter can give only one 12. The test is approximate in that respect.
    • The -final minimum leaves out newer participants and short-lived states.
    • The bias is an average over decades. Friendships that started or ended along the way are blurred.
    • Bootstrap intervals treat pairs as independent, but pairs that share a country are related, so the intervals are a little too narrow.
