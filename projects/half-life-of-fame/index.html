Half-life of fame

A film comes out, a public figure dies, a final is played, and for a day everybody reads about it. Then what? I took the most-read English Wikipedia articles of each month of , found the day each one peaked, and followed the days after. of them had a clear spike.

How it works, in plain words

No hand-picking

The articles are the 100 most-read of each month of 2025, as Wikipedia reports them. I did not choose the events.

Each article is its own yardstick

Its normal level is the median of the two months before the peak. Only views above that count as the spike.

Two clocks

Half-life measures the first drop. “Back near normal” measures the tail: the day views fall below twice the old level.

Two shapes

An exponential loses the same share every day. A power law drops fast and then lingers. I fitted both to each fade: the power law is closer in of them, so neither describes them all.

What this cannot tell you

  • Page views measure curiosity, not fame or importance, and only on English Wikipedia.
  • Views are daily, so a half-life under one day is an estimate between two points.
  • The kinds of article come from one-line descriptions matched with simple patterns. Some are misfiled, and the “other” group is a mix.
  • Only articles that reached a monthly top 100 are included. Small spikes are missing.
  • A second event can hit during the 90 days: a sequel, an award, a trial. That lengthens the tail and I do not remove it.
