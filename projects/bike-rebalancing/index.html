Discount or truck?

Bike-share stations run empty in one place while they fill up in another. The operator can pay a truck crew to move bikes, or offer riders a small discount to do it for them. I forecast every station’s hourly flow, built a discount rule on the forecast, and replayed real rides from October 2026 to see which costs less.

The short answer for this system: the truck. Discounts help, but they only pay off when they are cheap.

Predict

For every station and every hour I forecast departures and arrivals. The model is trained on April to August and judged on October. I compare it with two rules of thumb an operator could use without any model.

Decide and measure

Every hour, the rule flags stations likely to run empty or fill up. Riders heading to a station within 400 m of a flagged one are offered a discount to use the flagged one instead. Why the discounts fall short

  • of all failures happen between 07:00 and 10:00, when commuters ride to the trains and the docks there fill up. The worst station is . A rider on foot cannot fix that; the free docks are too far away.
  • It takes about discounts to avoid one failed ride, because many discounted rides would not have failed anyway. The truck avoids one with about bikes moved.
  • A better forecast would not change this much. With a perfect forecast the discounts avoid only more failed rides than with my model. The limit is the reach of the discount, not the prediction.
  • Used together they add up to a little more: truck plus discounts avoids of failures.

What this cannot tell you

  • The replay is a simulation. The trip records do not say how many bikes each station held, so every station starts each day half full.
  • The real rides happened in a city where crews were already moving bikes. A rider who found no bike in real life left no record, so true demand is higher than the data shows.
  • The share of riders who accept a discount is an assumption. I show 10% to 50% and you can switch between them.
  • Truck moves are instant here. A real truck needs time to drive and load, so the truck looks better than it would in practice.
  • The model uses the weather that actually happened. A live system would have to use a weather forecast.
