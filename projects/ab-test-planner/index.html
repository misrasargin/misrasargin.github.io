Did the promotion work?

A shop shows half its visitors a new offer and sales go up a little. Real effect, or luck? This page answers the two questions around that test: how long it has to run before you can tell, and what the numbers say once it is over. It also shows the mistake that turns one test in four into a fake winner.

How it works, in plain words

The question

Is the gap between two groups bigger than chance alone would produce?

The test

A two-sided test of two proportions. The planner turns it around: given the lift you care about, how many visitors make that gap stand out from chance.

Whole weeks

Shoppers behave differently on weekends, so the planner rounds the length up to full weeks.

Checked twice

The formulas on this page run in your browser. They are tested against SciPy, and against simulation.

What this cannot tell you

  • It assumes each visitor is counted once and decides independently. Returning visitors and shared carts break that.
  • It covers yes-or-no outcomes such as bought or not. Money per visitor needs a different test.
  • A new offer often gets a short burst of attention that fades. A test that is long enough on paper can still be too short for that.
  • “Not significant” does not mean “no effect”. Read the interval: it shows how large an effect is still possible.
